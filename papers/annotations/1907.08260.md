## 1907.08260 --- Moosmüller, Dietrich, Kevrekidis (2019)
**"A geometric approach to the transport of discontinuous densities"**
arXiv: 1907.08260 | physics.data-an

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems, optimal transport

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: The paper treats transport-map identification as the inference problem: given only source and target histograms (empirical densities), the transport plan between them is massively non-unique whenever the underlying relation is not bijective (folds create density singularities; marginalization loses observables). Density-only OT returns an arbitrary coupling among the admissible set. The contribution is to shrink that ambiguity by adding side information — so the matching is conditioned on structure beyond the two marginals, precisely the degeneracy of first-order OT that second-order/GW methods attack differently.
- **Stability** (weak): The hypothesis of a smooth manifold underlying the source-target relation is what makes recovery well-posed; the paper discusses limitations in recovering "the true" relation when the manifold folds over the observation spaces. Depth-limited: abstract frames this qualitatively, no perturbation theorem visible.
- **Parameterized homology** (weak): Not instantiated topologically, but the "observation process" — sampling short histories along the relation — plays the role of sweeping a parameter (history length) to reveal structure invisible at any single slice, cognate to how filtrations expose features a single scale misses.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Directional novelty relative to 2409.08768: there, dynamical-systems measure theory generalizes Takens and OT provides the language; here, attractor-reconstruction (delay vectors from short observation histories) is used as SIDE INFORMATION to disambiguate a transport problem. The bridge Dynamics↔Matching runs in both directions across these two papers, which is exactly what the thin cell needed.
- The observation-process device: augmenting each sample with its short past turns a marginal-coupling problem into one where the underlying manifold (graph-like relation) can be recovered even when the projection onto either space is many-to-one — delay embedding repurposed as a disambiguation tool outside dynamical systems proper.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Attractor reconstruction in dynamical systems (explicitly borrowed); standard optimal-transport practice in forward uncertainty quantification (as the practice being corrected).
