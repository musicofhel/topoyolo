## 1910.05979 --- Ay, Polani & Virgo (2019)
**"Information Decomposition based on Cooperative Game Theory"**
arXiv: 1910.05979 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: Core subject — decomposing I(T; X₁,…,Xₙ) into non-negative contributions of source combinations; explicitly an alternative account of what the joint description carries beyond its parts.
- **Matching**: The decomposition is imported from cooperative game theory as a *fair-share allocation* of mutual information to each coalition of sources — an assignment problem between information quantities and source subsets, solved by a value-allocation rule rather than Möbius inversion.
- **Chain complex**: Works on a DIFFERENT lattice from the Williams-Beer redundancy lattice — fewer terms, analogues of synergy and unique information but NO redundancy term. A second lattice over the same source-combination space, demonstrating the lattice is a design parameter, not a given.
- **Stability**: The payoff of dropping the redundancy term is axiomatic robustness: the decomposition simultaneously satisfies local positivity and identity, which the abstract states no PID measure can — a feasibility/statement about which axiom systems admit consistent solutions.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The impossibility contrast: positivity + identity are jointly satisfiable only OFF the standard lattice — evidence that the Williams-Beer lattice, not information theory, forces the trade-off. Directly atlas-relevant: same machine, incompatible axiom systems, resolved by changing the underlying order structure.
- Game-theoretic import path (coalitional fairness → information allocation) is a proof-technology bridge analogous to OT-as-technology elsewhere in the atlas.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: PID tradition (Williams-Beer lattice), cooperative game theory (fair-share/Shapley-style allocation). No cross-domain awareness. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
