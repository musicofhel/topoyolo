## 2303.15933 — Higgott & Gidney (2023)
**"Sparse Blossom: correcting a million errors per core second with minimum-weight matching"**

**Domain(s)**: Quantum error correction

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: The decoder problem IS the matching machine in its most operational form. Syndrome defects are odd-valued parity-check violations; correction is minimum-weight perfect matching of defect pairs on the syndrome graph. The contribution is an algorithmic sharpening of the blossom algorithm ("sparse blossom") that solves the QEC decoding problem directly, eliminating the all-to-all Dijkstra searches that generic MWPM implementations pay for — the matching is computed over a sparse neighborhood structure instead.
- **Stability**: The performance claim is stability-class: at 0.1% circuit-level depolarising noise the decoder sustains syndrome processing at hardware generation rate (distance-17 surface code, both X and Z bases, <1 μs per round per core) — reliable decoding held across the sustained stream of noisy rounds rather than per-shot.
- **Parameterized homology / phase transitions**: The operating point is stated against the surface-code threshold (0.1% well below threshold for circuit-level noise), i.e. the decoder functions inside the sub-threshold regime where logical error suppression is exponential.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Sparse blossom itself: a variant of Edmonds' blossom algorithm specialized to the structure of QEC syndrome graphs (defects on a lattice with known locality), replacing all-to-all shortest-path precomputation with local growth. This is a contribution to matching *algorithms*, not to the machine's semantics — but it demonstrates how much headroom the same abstract machine has when instantiated sparsely.
- Throughput as a first-class decoder metric: "matches the rate at which syndrome data is generated" — real-time decoding as the binding constraint, which pure accuracy analyses of MWPM do not capture.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: PyMatching v2 lineage (the authors' own v1), standard MWPM decoders for surface codes. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
