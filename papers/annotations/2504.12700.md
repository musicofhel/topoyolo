## 2504.12700 --- de Mello Koch & Ghosh (2025)
**"A Two-Phase Perspective on Deep Learning Dynamics"**
arXiv: 2504.12700 | hep-th, cs.LG

**Domain(s)**: machine learning, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Parameterized homology**: Training time is treated as a parameter along which the network's effective description changes kind — rapid curve-fitting then slow compression — and the paper proposes mutual information between hidden layers and inputs as the *progress measure* tracking what persists across that parameter, in the same role a filtration index plays for persistent features.
- **Stability**: The compression phase is argued to be "a principled form of forgetting, critical for generalization" — i.e. generalization is robustness of the learned function under perturbation of the input distribution, purchased by discarding fit-specific structure. What survives the coarse-graining phase is exactly what is stable.
- **Null hypothesis**: Grokking, double descent and the information bottleneck are all diagnosed against the null expectation "zero training error ⇒ done": delayed generalization is detected precisely as an excess over what fitting alone predicts, and the paper's claim is the three phenomena share this deviation structure.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Unifying grokking / double descent / information bottleneck by *shared timescale alignment* across two different settings, rather than by any shared mechanism claim.
- The negative result that the second (compression) phase is not actively optimized by standard training algorithms and may be unnecessarily prolonged — an RG-flavored diagnosis that SGD leaves the useful coarse-graining unfinished.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Information bottleneck (Tishby), grokking, double descent, local complexity and linear-mapping-number circuit metrics, renormalization-group analogy.
