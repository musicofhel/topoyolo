## cond-mat/9908104 --- Kabashima, Murayama & Saad (1999)
**"Typical Performance of Gallager-type Error-Correcting Codes"**
arXiv: cond-mat/9908104 | cond-mat.dis-nn

**Domain(s)**: Information theory, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Null hypothesis**: Shannon capacity is the canonical destroyed-structure reference for coding: the paper shows many Gallager-type code families saturate it (typical-case performance reaches the information-theoretic optimum), while others sit slightly below — making explicit which structural choices of the sparse matrices cost performance relative to the null.

- **Parameterized homology**: Performance is tracked across a family of codes indexed by the number of non-zero row/column elements in the random sparse matrices — an invariant (typical decoded error rate) studied as a function of that structural parameter, with families separating into capacity-saturating and sub-capacity classes.

- **Stability**: The equivalence shown between TAP decoding and belief-propagation decoding means results proved under one mean-field formalism transfer verbatim to the other — a robustness-of-analysis statement: the phenomenology does not depend on which statistical-physics lens is used.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Statistical-physics typical-case analysis of Gallager's product-of-bits construction showing which members of the sparse-matrix family actually saturate Shannon capacity.
2. The TAP ≡ belief-propagation decoding identification, letting cavity/TAP machinery be read directly as the practical decoder.
3. Early (pre-modern-LDPC) instance of the pattern the atlas tracks everywhere: phase-diagram methods locating where coding schemes hit or miss the information-theoretic bound.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Gallager's original low-density codes; TAP approach; belief-propagation decoding. Direct lineage to the batch-004 ldpc-bp group (`annotations/1307.5210.md`, `annotations/cond-mat-9908358.md`). No TDA/OT content.
