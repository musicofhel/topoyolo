## 10.1111/2041-210X.13985 --- Caruso, Clemente, Rillig, Garlaschelli (2021)
**"Fluctuating ecological networks: A synthesis of maximum-entropy approaches for pattern detection and process inference"**
DOI: 10.1111/2041-210X.13985 | Methods in Ecology and Evolution

**Domain(s)**: Information theory, dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The paper's object is the null model itself — maximum-entropy ensembles of ecological networks (food webs, plant–pollinator systems) that randomize link structure while enforcing observed constraints, so that any non-random pattern is measured as departure from the constrained-random ensemble. The methodological thesis is that the null must be built honestly: classical ecological nulls treat node-level properties (species degree, interaction totals) as 'hard' constraints fixed exactly, which bakes the signal into the surrogate and miscalibrates the test.
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: The hard-vs-soft distinction is precisely a joint-vs-marginal construction. Soft constraints enforce the constraint values (the marginal statistics — degree sequences, interaction strengths) only in expectation over the ensemble, while allowing fluctuations around them; the excess structure lives in how the realized joint configuration deviates from what its own marginals pin down. Detecting nestedness, modularity, or robustness "above the null" means measuring joint structure beyond what the enforced local (marginal) statistics explain.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Synthesis transferring the statistical-physics max-ent null-model toolkit (Garlaschelli lineage) into mainstream ecology practice, with open-source code.
- The soft-constraint reformulation: constraints allowed to fluctuate rather than fixed, giving statistically calibrated pattern detection under interspecific heterogeneity and for weighted (quantitative) networks.
- Explicit treatment of spatio-temporal fluctuation of global properties (total interactions) alongside node-level ones.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Maximum-entropy network ensembles and hidden-variable models (Garlaschelli, Squartini), classical ecological null models, robustness and nestedness literature. No connections to TDA, QEC, or neuroscience.
