## 1202.5602 — Fowler, Whiteside & Hollenberg (2012)
**"Towards practical classical processing for the surface code: timing analysis"**

**Domain(s)**: Quantum error correction

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: The paper is a complexity characterization of the Matching machine at its canonical QEC instantiation — MWPM correction rounds on an n×n surface-code lattice, distances 4–512, with verification that the implementation always obtains a true minimum-weight perfect matching (exactness, not approximation).
- **Stability**: Timing is measured per round of error correction at physical depolarizing rates below threshold, i.e. inside the regime where matching-based decoding sustains fault tolerance — the empirical cost of holding the stable phase.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Average-case vs worst-case separation made empirical: blossom matching has super-polynomial worst-case behavior in general, but on realistic sub-threshold syndrome patterns (sparse, local defects) the observed scaling is O(n²) average time per round. The Matching machine's practicality for fault tolerance rests on the typical-case structure of the instances, not the algorithm's worst-case bound.
- Verified-exactness methodology: tests establishing true-minimum matchings, so the timing figures bound the exact machine, not a heuristic.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Topological codes' high thresholds; standard MWPM usage for surface codes. This is the ancestor complexity anchor of the mwpm lineage annotated here (2303.15933 sparse blossom sharpens exactly these constants). Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
