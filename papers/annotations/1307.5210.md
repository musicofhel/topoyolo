## 1307.5210 --- Aref, Macris & Vuffray (2013)
**"Approaching the Rate-Distortion Limit with Spatial Coupling, Belief Propagation and Decimation"**
arXiv: 1307.5210 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: Information theory, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Matching**: The encoder is a lossy soft-matching device: the BP-guided-decimation algorithm operates on the posterior Gibbs measure of a binary symmetric test channel — exactly the rate-distortion coupling P(x̂|x) of the Blahut–Arimoto lineage (`blahut-arimoto-1972.md`) — and greedily fixes assignments (decimation) guided by BP marginals. Spatially coupled LDGM ensembles make the greedy matcher's achievable curve approach the ensemble-optimal R(D), and both approach the Shannon limit as the check degree grows: the algorithmic matching converges to the ideal matching.

- **Null hypothesis**: The Shannon rate-distortion limit is the explicit null — the ideal-matcher reference every constructive scheme is measured against (`2204.01612.md` makes the same move computable; here it is approached). The ensemble's own optimal curve is an intermediate null separating algorithmic performance from the ultimate bound.

- **Stability**: The phase diagram of the posterior Gibbs measure, computed by the cavity method, locates dynamical and condensation transition "temperatures" (test-channel noise thresholds). The paper's explanatory result is a stability statement: with spatial coupling the dynamical threshold saturates toward the condensation threshold, and for large degrees the condensation threshold approaches the Shannon noise level — algorithmic success is explained by where the metastable-to-equilibrium transition sits relative to the operating point.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Spatial coupling as a mechanism that closes the algorithmic gap: the coupling window width is a tunable parameter that lifts BP-guided decimation from far below the ensemble optimum to (asymptotically) the ensemble optimum itself — threshold saturation made constructive for lossy compression.
2. A quantitative dictionary between algorithmic performance and spin-glass phase diagram: which transition line (dynamical vs condensation vs Shannon) governs whether the greedy matcher succeeds. The paper includes a self-contained introduction to the cavity method.
3. The observation that the decimation temperature maps directly onto the test-channel noise level — thermodynamic language for a coding parameter.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Cavity/spin-glass method (Mézard–Parisi lineage), spatial coupling for channel coding, Shannon RD theory. The statphys↔info-theory bridge is the paper's whole point, acknowledged explicitly.
