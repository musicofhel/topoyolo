## 1306.4064 — Small, Judd, Stemler (2013)
**"A surrogate for networks -- How scale-free is my scale-free network?"**

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: Transplant of the surrogate machine from time series to complex networks: generate "statistically likely" networks consistent with both the observed data and a chosen null (e.g. a particular degree distribution), then ask which measured properties are typical of the null ensemble and which are peculiar to the observation. Same epistemics as nlin/0009032: a peculiarity verdict licenses only the negation of the constructed null.
- **Stability**: "Typical vs peculiar" is precisely a stability statement — a property is stable if it survives regenerating the network under the null's constraints; peculiar properties are those unstable under the null-preserving regeneration.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The carrier change (series → network): what plays the role of "phase randomization" for a graph is a new generator-design problem; the paper's methods for sampling networks matching data + null are the contribution.
- Directly interrogates the scale-free claim itself — using null ensembles to show which power-law observations are unremarkable.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: THE rare explicit cross-domain bridge in this group — the abstract states "a direct analogy between the approach we propose here and the surrogate data methods applied to nonlinear time series data." Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
