## 2312.03489 --- Sekizawa, Ito, Oizumi (2023)
**"Decomposing Thermodynamic Dissipation of Linear Langevin Systems via Oscillatory Modes and Its Application to Neural Dynamics"**
arXiv: 2312.03489 | q-bio.NC/cond-mat.stat-mech

**Domain(s)**: Statistical physics, neuroscience, dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Joint-vs-marginal** (instrumental): The central result is a decomposition — housekeeping entropy production splits into independent *positive* contributions from oscillatory modes (and, for diagonal noise, per system element). This is the composite-systems move applied to the mode structure of a linear Langevin system rather than to spatial subsystems: total dissipation ≥ sum over mode-attributable parts, with each part individually meaningful. Positivity is what makes it an attribution story rather than a basis change.
- **Filtration / parameterization** (weak): The frequency band plays the role of the parameter index — delta, theta, alpha bands are slices through the oscillatory spectrum, and the decomposition makes each band's dissipation share a well-defined observable, tracked across conditions (awake vs anesthetized). A band-resolved dissipation profile is a persistence-like curve over frequency.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The bridge itself: this is the second explicit Neuroscience↔Statistical-physics instantiation in batch-004 (after 1810.09545), and it is *empirical* — monkey ECoG under awake vs anesthesia, with consistent cross-animal trends (delta-band contribution larger under anesthesia; theta smaller). Neural oscillations, the most-studied phenomenology in the atlas's Neuroscience column, get a thermodynamic reading via physical limits of information processing.
- Interpretive payoff: changes in neural oscillation across brain states become statements about where dissipation lives in the mode spectrum — a semantics for oscillation that neither the dynamical-systems nor the neuroscience columns currently carry.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Stochastic thermodynamics (entropy production rate, housekeeping vs excess EP); linear response / Langevin theory; neuroscience ECoG literature; information-processing limits.
