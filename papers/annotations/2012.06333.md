## 2012.06333 --- Hansen & Gebhart (2020)
**"Sheaf Neural Networks"**
arXiv: 2012.06333 | cs.LG, math.AT

**Domain(s)**: TDA, machine learning

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Chain complex**: The sheaf Laplacian is built from cellular-sheaf coboundary data on a graph: each node carries a stalk (vector space) and each edge a restriction map, so diffusion operates on the cochain complex C⁰ → C¹ rather than on bare node features. The graph Laplacian L = BᵀB is recovered exactly as the special case of constant stalks and identity restrictions; the general object is the Hodge-style Laplacian δᵀδ of the graph's incidence chain complex, with non-constant, asymmetric, dimension-varying restriction maps.
- **Matching**: The learned restriction maps assign to each edge its own linear "translation" between endpoint stalk spaces — a per-edge correspondence problem solved jointly with representation learning (the same role optimal transport plays between metric spaces, here algebraized).
- **Stability**: The paper's empirical claim is that encoding relational structure in the Laplacian makes learning robust where relations are asymmetric and signed — cases where the symmetric constant-stalk Laplacian loses exactly the signal that distinguishes classes.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The founding move of the sheaf-NN lineage: promoting restriction maps from fixed structure to *learnable parameters*, so the chain-complex machine becomes an architecture whose geometry is fitted to data. Prior corpus entries use sheaf/Laplacian machinery with given coefficients; here the boundary data itself is trained.
- Dimension-varying stalks: nodes need not share feature dimension — the complex is not homogeneous, which no other corpus chain-complex instantiation does at the architecture level.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Generalizes GCNs; cites cellular sheaf theory (Curry, Shepard). No TDA-persistence or Rosetta-domain awareness — ML/math silo.
