## 2505.23993 --- Hu (2025)
**"Cellular Sheaves on Higher-Dimensional Structures"**
arXiv: 2505.23993 | math.AT

**Domain(s)**: TDA, mathematics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Chain complex**: Extends cellular sheaves from graphs (C⁰ → C¹) to full simplicial complexes, so sheaf coboundaries live on every graded level C⁰ → C¹ → C² → …; higher sheaf Laplacians then encode multi-way (triangle-and-above) interactions rather than pairwise ones — the same graded boundary-operator stack as simplicial Hodge theory, with sheaf-valued coefficients.
- **Stability**: The 0th sheaf Laplacian is shown to recover the anisotropic network model (ANM) Hessian — the elastic stiffness matrix of a protein structure network — so the paper's geometric constructions inherit ANM's physical robustness reading: the Laplacian's low modes are the mechanically soft directions of the structure.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The construction problem itself: prior corpus sheaf entries (2012.06333, 2511.11092) all operate on graphs where the incidence structure is given for free. This paper treats "what is a non-trivial sheaf above dimension 1" as the open question, and gives two independent answers: a geometric-physical one (stalks/restrictions motivated by ANM Hessians, recovering classical elasticity data as a special case) and an algebraic one (ringed spaces, sheaves of ideals and modules over commutative algebra).
- The algebraic branch is a genuinely different incarnation than anything in the corpus: sheaf structure defined by ideals/modules rather than by linear restriction maps between vector spaces — a combinatorial-algebraic grounding where the "coboundary" is algebraic localization/quotient data, not a matrix.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Explicitly frames itself within TDA and sheaf neural network development; cites anisotropic network models from computational biology. No awareness of the Rosetta's other domains.
