## 1601.00716 --- Cobey & Baskerville (2016)
**"Limits to causal inference with state-space reconstruction for infectious disease"**
arXiv: 1601.00716 | q-bio.QM

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: A systematic power/limits study of the CCM (convergent cross mapping) test — the state-space-reconstruction null machinery's flagship — showing its wrong-alternative firing rate is extreme in practice: independent strains oscillating at similar frequencies are inferred as interacting (shared-periodicity false positives). This is the Timmer-lineage move applied to CCM: quantify when the test rejects a true null, not merely when it succeeds.
- **Stability**: The verdicts are shown unstable along three perturbation directions of the data-generating process — process noise level, attractor drift, and embedding/attractor-reconstruction quality — none of which are measurable in natural systems. Instability of inference under unobservable perturbations is the paper's headline.
- **Matching**: Cross mapping itself is nearest-neighbor matching between reconstructed state spaces; the critique is that match quality (and hence the statistic) inherits artifacts of the reconstruction rather than coupling strength.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The demonstration that CCM's central failure mode is *shared periodicity* — a spectral coincidence, not a dynamical coupling — and that alternative inference criteria dissolve this specific sensitivity while noise/drift sensitivity remains. The failure is diagnosable and partially fixable, which distinguishes it from a blanket rejection.
- The epidemiological grounding: pre-vaccine measles/pertussis series from NYC and Chicago show that the assumptions CCM needs are unverifiable exactly where the method is most tempting.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Cites Sugihara et al. (CCM), Granger causality literature, surrogate-data testing implicitly via reconstruction diagnostics. No TDA/QEC awareness.
