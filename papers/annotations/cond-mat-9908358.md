## cond-mat/9908358 --- Vicente, Saad & Kabashima (1999)
**"Statistical Physics of Irregular Low-Density Parity-Check Codes"**
arXiv: cond-mat/9908358 | cond-mat.dis-nn

**Domain(s)**: Information theory, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Null hypothesis**: Shannon's coding bound is the explicit null — the replica computation locates a phase transition that, with appropriately chosen irregularity parameters, *coincides* with the bound. The ideal-channel reference is not merely a benchmark here but the predicted location of an actual thermodynamic transition: capacity-approaching ensembles are those whose decoding transition sits on the null.

- **Stability**: The replica analysis gives the error-free decoding region of the irregular ensemble as a phase in the (noise, rate) plane; the transition line is the stability boundary of the decoded fixed point. Irregularity is shown to be a mechanism for moving that boundary outward — degree-distribution optimization as stability engineering.

- **Matching**: BP decoding is analyzed with statistical-physics arguments and its theoretical performance curve is checked against simulation — message passing as approximate soft matching between received words and codewords, with the replica solution supplying the ideal-matcher reference against which BP's gap is measured. The regular-vs-irregular comparison isolates what contributes to closing the algorithmic-to-optimal gap.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Early (1999) demonstration that the *typical-case* performance of practical codes is a spin-glass thermodynamic quantity: the replica method applied to irregular LDPC ensembles predates and seeds the cavity-method coding literature that `1307.5210.md` later makes constructive.
2. Degree-distribution irregularity identified as the structural parameter controlling where the decoding transition sits relative to the Shannon null — an early instance of "ensemble design = threshold placement."
3. Direct comparison of replica-theoretic predictions with finite-size simulations, establishing the statistical-physics machinery as quantitatively (not just qualitatively) predictive for decoding.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Shannon theory (bound as transition point), belief propagation (decoding dynamics), spin-glass/replica method (analysis tool). The statphys↔info-theory bridge is explicit and constitutive of the paper. No TDA content.
