## 1903.05124 --- Choi, Bao, Qi & Altman (2019)
**"Quantum Error Correction in Scrambling Dynamics and Measurement-Induced Phase Transition"**
arXiv: 1903.05124 | quant-ph, cond-mat.str-el

**Domain(s)**: Quantum, statistical physics, information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: The central claim is that error correction — a joint property of the many-body state, defined by whether logical information is recoverable from a subregion — is the right lens on measurement-induced entanglement transitions. Whether information survives in the joint unitary+measurement dynamics is not decidable from any local or marginal description of the trajectory; the volume-law phase stores information non-locally across the system in exactly the QEC sense.

- **Parameterized homology**: The paper's phase diagram is a capacity curve tracked through two control parameters — measurement rate and scrambling degree. Entanglement entropy scaling (volume-law ↔ area-law) changes sharply as the measurement rate crosses an error threshold; this is the canonical "invariant changes at critical parameter value" structure, with the invariant being entanglement scaling and later refinements making it order-parameter-like.

- **Stability**: The two phases are stable: chaotic unitary evolution actively protects quantum information against projective measurements acting as errors, up to a threshold rate. The QEC code distance / protection margin is precisely what degrades under increasing measurement perturbation, giving quantitative robustness of the encoded (topological-like) information.

- **Null hypothesis**: The area-law phase is the destroyed-structure reference: above-threshold measurements erase the long-range entanglement so that steady states become indistinguishable (for information purposes) from short-range-entangled states with no hidden logical content. The transition marks where the null becomes observationally inescapable.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. The identification: measurement-induced entanglement transitions ARE quantum error correction transitions — the chaotic dynamics acts as an encoding, measurements as errors, and the critical measurement rate as a code-dependent threshold. This recasts a many-body dynamical phenomenon as an information-protection problem.
2. The explicit link between the entanglement phase transition and changes in *quantum channel capacity* of the monitored evolution — capacity itself displays a phase transition as measurement rate varies.
3. A solvable random-circuit model with independently tunable scrambling and measurement rates, with numerically obtained phase diagrams — the template for the entire subsequent MIPT literature.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Random unitary circuits and operator scrambling (Hayden–Preskill lineage), quantum error correcting codes, earlier work on entanglement growth in monitored systems. Explicit quantum-information ↔ condensed-matter bridge; no TDA or OT machinery.
