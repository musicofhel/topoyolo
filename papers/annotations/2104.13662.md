## 2104.13662 --- Theis & Wagner (2021)
**"A Coding Theorem for the Rate-Distortion-Perception Function"**
arXiv: 2104.13662 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: Information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Matching**: The rate-distortion-perception function adds a second coupling constraint to the classical problem: the reconstruction marginal P_X̂ must match the source marginal P_X (perception), on top of the expected-distortion constraint on the joint P(X,X̂). Where Blahut–Arimoto optimize a soft assignment under one constraint on the coupling's cost, RDPF optimization requires the coupling's *marginals to agree* — a distributional matching condition in the optimal-transport mold (the perception term is precisely a divergence between marginals, the OT-style constraint the atlas files under Matching).

- **Null hypothesis** (weak): The coding theorem itself is a converse-flavored benchmark: RDPF lower-bounds the achievable rate for stochastic variable-length codes, so it serves as the ideal-matcher null for realism-constrained codecs — the same role R(D) plays unconstrained.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. First achievability result: the RDPF is not merely a variational definition but is operationally achievable, using stochastic variable-length codes built on Li–El Gamal's likelihood coder.
2. Establishes that the realism/distortion trade-off studied via the Blau–Michaeli variational function corresponds to real codes — closing the definitional-vs-operational gap left open when the RDPF was introduced.
3. The perceptual quality criterion ("reconstructions indistinguishable in distribution from sources") is given an exact operational semantics rather than an adversarial-discriminator proxy.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Blau & Michaeli (2019, RDPF definition), Li & El Gamal (2018), classical Shannon source coding.
