## 2108.04074 --- Röhm, Gauthier, Fischer (2021)
**"Model-free inference of unseen attractors"**
arXiv: 2108.04074 | cs.LG

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: An RC trained on data sampled near *one* co-existing attractor of a multistable Lorenz-like system builds a state-space representation rich enough to carry the *other* basins: the trained reservoir's autonomous trajectories land on attractors never visited during training. The correspondence learned from partial samples extends to unsampled regions — generalization here is a claim about the reconstruction map covering more phase space than its training distribution did.
- **Stability**: The mechanism is that the RC approximates the local flow well enough that its global orbit structure is inherited, not memorized; inference of unseen attractors is robustness of the learned vector field under extrapolation away from the training support. (Depth-limited: abstract asserts capability; no quantitative extrapolation modulus visible.)
- **Null hypothesis**: "Unseen attractor found" is checked against the null that the reservoir merely interpolates training trajectories — prediction of an attractor whose basin was never approached is the rejection case.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Direction of the generalization claim: prior reservoir-reconstruction work (`annotations/2108.05024.md`, `annotations/2401.00885.md`) asks whether the *sampled* attractor is reproduced faithfully; this paper asks whether attractors *outside* the training sample are recovered — reconstruction validated on unsampled phase space, from a single noisy trajectory.
- Multistability as the testbed: co-existing attractors make "phase-space coverage" a sharp, binary observable (either the unseen attractor appears in autonomous evolution or it does not).

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: reservoir computing for chaotic time series, echo-state property lineage, multistability in Lorenz-type systems. No TDA, QEC, or OT.
