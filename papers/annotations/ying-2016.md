## ying-2016 — Ying, Pan, Fox, Agarwal (2016)
**"A Simple Efficient Approximation Algorithm for Dynamic Time Warping"**

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems (trajectory analysis), computational geometry

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: DTW is exactly a constrained optimal assignment: find the minimum-cost monotone (non-crossing) set of correspondences between two point sequences such that every point is matched at least once. The standard solver is a minimum-weight path in the DP grid graph — an assignment problem with a monotonicity (order-preserving) side constraint, not a general bipartite matching.
- **Stability**: The paper's guarantee is a robustness bound on the matching value: the returned correspondence set costs at most (1+ε)·dtw(P,Q) — the optimal assignment cost is stable under algorithmic approximation. The κ-packedness condition (curve intersects any radius-r disk in length ≤ κr) plus bounded spread σ are precisely structural regularity conditions under which this stable fast matching is possible; without them no subquadratic guarantee holds.
- **Null hypothesis**: Implicit only. The DTW cost itself acts as the statistic distinguishing "same underlying curve" from "unrelated sequences"; the paper provides machinery for computing it, not for calibrating it against a destroyed-structure reference.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- A considerably simpler O(κ²/ε · n log σ) approximation algorithm than prior work [1], with practical implementations: order-of-magnitude speedup over exact O(mn) DP at n ≥ 5,000 points, error within 5–10%.
- The complexity parameterization itself is the contribution: running time governed by packing constant κ and spread σ — i.e., matching cost depends on geometric regularity of the underlying continua, not just sample count.
- Applications as subroutine (k-nearest trajectory retrieval, iterative closest point) with 8–12× speedups. No topological invariants, no filtration, no information-theoretic quantity appears anywhere.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Cite Alt & Braunmutl's continuous DTW work and the prior κ-packed approximation result [1]; situated in computational geometry (curve matching) and GIS trajectory analysis. No connection acknowledged to TDA, QEC, neuroscience, or information theory — despite DTW's role in neuroscience GC pipelines (see Amornbunchornvej 2020 in composite_systems).
