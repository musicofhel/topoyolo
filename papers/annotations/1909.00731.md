## 1909.00731 --- Barraquand, Picoche, Detto & Hartig (2019)
**"Inferring species interactions using Granger causality and convergent cross mapping"**
arXiv: 1909.00731 | q-bio.PE

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The whole paper is an empirical calibration study of the GC-vs-CCM methodological divide: the folklore claim was that CCM (nonparametric, built for deterministic chaos) should beat linear Granger causality as dynamics grow nonlinear. Across predator–prey cycles, chaotic and stochastic 2-species competition, and 10–20-species networks, no correspondence between nonlinearity degree and which method wins — i.e. the "nonlinearity ⇒ use CCM" rule fails its own null test.
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: Weakly present — both methods detect directed interaction as predictive structure in the coupled system beyond what each series' own autoregressive model explains; the paper's point is that this excess is method-robust, not method-specific.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- A wrong-alternative study of a *method-choice heuristic* rather than of either method: it refutes the assumed map from dynamical regime (deterministic-chaotic vs stochastic) to optimal inference machinery. This is the same move as Cobey–Baskerville's Timmer-class critique (`annotations/1601.00716.md`) but aimed at the selection rule instead of at CCM's power — and it lands on the conciliatory side: linear MAR(p) Granger is declared valid even for nonlinear ecological networks.
- Ecological-network scale evidence (10- and 20-species interaction networks) rather than toy coupled maps — performance equivalence demonstrated at network scale, not just pairwise.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Explicitly frames GC and CCM as two causal-inference traditions (economics-born vs dynamical-systems-born) applied to the same task; recommends choosing by analysis aims rather than by assumed nonlinearity. No Rosetta-cross-domain awareness.
