## chao-dyn/9807039 — Timmer (1998)
**"The power of surrogate data testing with respect to non-stationarity"**

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: Quantifies the failure mode of the surrogate null: because the tested null is "linear, Gaussian, stationary", a positive outcome can come from a linear *non-stationary* process just as well as from a nonlinear one. The paper computes the test's power against non-stationarity specifically — i.e. it measures how often the null machine fires on the wrong member of the null's own complement.
- **Stability**: Power analysis = stability study of the test verdict under perturbation of the true process away from the null along a specified direction (stationarity broken, linearity kept). The reject/non-reject boundary is shown to be crossed by linear drifts, so the verdict is not stable in the direction practitioners care about.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Turning the generic caveat ("a rejection may reflect non-stationarity") into a quantitative power curve: how detectable non-stationarity is depends on the preserved statistics of the chosen surrogate algorithm.
- Concrete instantiation of the Timmer 2000 epistemic critique (nlin/0009032): the wrong-alternative detection rate is not hand-waved but computed.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Theiler et al. (1992) surrogate framework; nonlinear time series practice that treats positive tests as evidence of chaos. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
