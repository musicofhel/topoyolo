## 2006.12287 --- Weitkamp, Proksch, Tameling & Munk (2020)
**"Gromov-Wasserstein Distance based Object Matching: Asymptotic Inference"**
arXiv: 2006.12287 | math.ST

**Domain(s)**: TDA

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Matching**: Objects are modeled as metric measure spaces and compared by the Gromov-Wasserstein distance — the coupling-based soft assignment between internal-distance structures that is the Rosetta Matching machine in its purest form (no shared ground space, matching defined purely through intra-object relations).

- **Null hypothesis**: The paper's actual contribution is turning the matching functional into a *test*: pose-invariant object discrimination via a beta-trimmed lower bound of the GW distance, with distributional limits under the null. The reference distribution is constructed exactly as the machine prescribes — the statistic's sampling law under "the two objects are isomorphic up to pose" — so rejection is a statement about genuine relational mismatch.

- **Stability**: Weak convergence of a novel U-type process indexed by β ∈ [0, 1/2) gives the trimmed bound continuous, finite-sample-valid behavior — stability of the matching functional as a statistical object (distribution-level robustness rather than a perturbation inequality).

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Distributional limits for GW-type statistics: prior corpus entries use GW as an estimator or loss; this supplies its *sampling theory* (U-process weak convergence), which no abstract-machine framing generates for free.
2. Trimming (β) as a robustness dial inside the matching functional itself — the lower bound over couplings becomes controllably insensitive to outlier mass, a mechanism orthogonal to perturbation-stability inequalities.
3. Applied validation on structural protein comparison; nothing instantiates chain complex, parameterized homology, or joint-vs-marginal excess.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Situated entirely within OT/statistics (Sturm's GW, trimmed OT). No cross-domain engagement — but it is the natural inference-theoretic counterpart of the atlas's PH null-testing entries (permutation/max-ent lineages): same machine, different substrate.
