## 1101.6063 — Guarin, Delgado, Orozco (2011)
**"Band-phase-randomized Surrogates to assess nonlinearity in non-stationary time series"**

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: Repairs the surrogate null for non-stationary data by making it *partially* destructive: only Fourier phases in a high frequency band are randomized, so the constructed null is "linear with the observed low-band (non-stationarity-carrying) structure". A rejection then licenses "nonlinear beyond what low-band linear drift explains" — narrowing exactly the falsification/confirmation gap Timmer (2000) named.
- **Stability**: The preserved low band is a stability invariant by construction: the surrogate ensemble holds the non-stationary trend fixed while perturbing high-band phase structure, isolating the test verdict from drift-induced rejections (the power-loss failure mode of chao-dyn/9807039).

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The band-splitting move itself: choosing WHICH spectral region carries the null's preserved structure vs its destroyed structure is a design decision outside the six machines — the machines say "preserve X, destroy Y", not how to carve the spectrum.
- Validation on HRV signals with spikes/artefacts, where existing surrogates cannot reproduce the data's linear correlations at all.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Linear surrogate data methods (Theiler et al. 1992 lineage) and their stationarity dependence. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
