## 1705.07855 — Baireuther, O'Brien, Tarasinski & Beenakker (2017)
**"Machine-learning-assisted correction of correlated qubit errors in a topological code"**

**Domain(s)**: Quantum error correction

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: The baseline being beaten is the Matching machine in its canonical QEC form — MWPM on the surface-code syndrome graph. The paper's contribution is defined relationally to that machine: an RNN decoder trained on experimentally accessible syndrome data that outperforms the matching decoder.
- **Joint-vs-marginal (composite systems)**: The source of the gain is explicitly joint structure — correlations between bit-flip (X) and phase-flip errors that the matching decoder treats as independent X and Z matchings. The neural decoder detects the joint error distribution where MWPM factorizes it into two marginal problems; this is the joint-vs-marginal excess expressed as decoder advantage rather than information quantity.
- **Null hypothesis**: The RNN is trained only on experimentally accessible data and adapts to the physical system without a noise model — the learned decoder serves as its own implicit null model of the error process, in contrast to model-based decoders that assume one.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The demonstration that the joint-vs-marginal gain over matching can be captured by a learned recurrent decoder with no explicit correlation model: the LSTM layers maintain performance across many QEC cycles, so the joint structure is temporal (error memory) as well as across X/Z type.
- Training from experimentally accessible data only — decoupling decoder performance from noise-model fidelity.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Established MWPM/blossom decoding as the performance benchmark; neural-network QEC proposals preceding it. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
