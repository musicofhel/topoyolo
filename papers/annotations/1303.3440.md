## 1303.3440 --- Lizier, Flecker & Williams (2013)
**"Towards a Synergy-based Approach to Measuring Information Modification"**
arXiv: 1303.3440 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: information theory, complex systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: The paper's proposed measure of information modification is built directly on PID synergy: a modification event IS an interaction between two or more information sources that no single source carries — i.e. the joint description contains structure the marginals cannot supply. Glider/particle collisions in cellular automata become the canonical empirical signature of this excess, replacing the earlier heuristic "separable information".
- **Chain complex**: Applying the PID lattice (antichain poset + Möbius-style atom recovery) to distributed computation means decomposing each local computation step over the same graded-poset skeleton the corpus inherits from 1004.2515 — here the atoms are read as component operations on information (storage / transfer / modification) rather than as static channel properties.
- **Matching**: The localizability axiom is a worst-case-over-sources requirement: for modification to be measurable at space-time points, redundancy must be certifiable LOCALLY (per realisation, per source pair), not merely on average — a min-flavoured, per-outcome aggregation constraint on admissible redundancy measures, kin to the matching-side preference for pointwise couplings over averaged ones.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- A PID-grounded definition of information modification events (as opposed to storage and transfer), and the program of localizing them in space-time.
- The localizability axiom itself, plus the negative survey result: existing redundancy measures largely fail it — an explicit open-axiom gap in the lattice literature.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Partial information decomposition (Williams & Beer 2010), separable information heuristic, cellular-automata distributed computation (storage/transfer/modification taxonomy).
