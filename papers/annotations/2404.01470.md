## 2404.01470 --- Milzman (2024)
**"Measuring the Redundancy of Information from a Source Failure Perspective"**
arXiv: 2404.01470 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: information theory, quantum error correction

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Chain complex**: The paper's central object is an order-reversing correspondence between collections of source-fallible instantiations of a system and the Williams–Beer redundancy lattice: as you move DOWN the lattice (finer antichains, less redundant atoms), the class of single-source failures a piece of information survives GROWS. This is an anti-monotone map between two graded posets — the same lattice-plus-order-dual skeleton the corpus has been reading as chain-complex-like since 1004.2515, here with a second lattice attached by an explicit Galois-style correspondence.
- **Boundary operators**: The new measure I_ft is defined operationally: redundant information is exactly the information that survives individual source failures. "Redundant atom" is thereby re-grounded as a boundary/codimension notion — what persists when one coordinate is deleted — making the fault-tolerance side (erasure events) and the PID side (lattice atoms) two descriptions of one boundary structure.
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: I_ft satisfies the common Williams–Beer PID axioms, so the redundancy/synergy split it inherits is precisely joint-vs-marginal bookkeeping; the contribution is that the redundant direction now carries a failure-robustness semantics rather than only an overlap-of-information one.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The order-reversing correspondence between source-fallible instantiation collections and the PID lattice — a bridge from axiomatic PID to engineering fault tolerance that had no prior instance in the corpus.
- I_ft itself: a redundancy measure whose defining axiom is robustness to erasure rather than agreement across sources.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Partial information decomposition (Williams & Beer 2010), McGill's interaction information (1954), fault-tolerant engineering practice for sensing/routing/control. The QEC adjacency noted in the queue description is real but implicit — the paper works with generic component failures, not stabilizer codes; filed under quantum error correction as a cross-domain feed for the thin QEC × redundancy cell (cf. 1903.05124's MIPT-as-QEC-transition).
