## 2603.05381 — Hack, Menti, Lazaro & Paler (2026)
**"Achieving Thresholds via Standalone Belief Propagation on Surface Codes"**

**Domain(s)**: Quantum error correction

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Chain complex / message passing**: Standard BP for QEC runs on the Tanner graph of the code and provably fails to reach threshold on the surface code. This paper moves message passing onto the *decoding graph* — the same graph object the Matching machine operates on — and obtains standalone-BP thresholds close to MWPM under depolarizing noise. The message-passing machine and the assignment machine are shown to be interchangeable at fixed carrier graph.
- **Stability / phase transitions**: The claim is threshold-class: standalone BP attains code-capacity thresholds for the surface code, i.e. the decoder crosses into the sub-threshold regime that Tanner-graph BP could not reach. Threshold equality with MWPM is the stability statement.
- **Matching**: The decoding-graph formulation keeps the paper MWPM-compatible "for any graphlike QEC code" — the contribution is positioned as replacing minimum-weight assignment with local messages while targeting the identical graph object, opening hardware-accelerated implementations of MWPM-compatible decoders.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Locating the failure of Tanner-graph BP in the *carrier graph choice*, not the message-passing algorithm itself: exchanging messages on the decoding graph rescues BP where it was known not to have a threshold. This is a clean instance of two abstract machines (local message passing, global perfect matching) sharing a substrate.
- Practical consequence: BP becomes drop-in replaceable with/against MWPM in the same hardware pipeline.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Conventional Tanner-graph BP decoders; MWPM as both threshold benchmark and compatibility target. Lineage: the ldpc-bp group (Kabashima–Murayama–Saad cond-mat/9908104; Jain–Koehler–Liu–Mossel 1905.10031) established BP-vs-assignment transitions on sparse codes. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
