## 0910.0805 --- Touboul & Destexhe (2009)
**"Can power-law scaling and neuronal avalanches arise from stochastic dynamics?"**
arXiv: 0910.0805 | nlin.AO

**Domain(s)**: neuroscience, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The core machine, aimed at an evidence procedure: thresholded stochastic processes generically produce apparent power-law event-size distributions under log-log regression — the scaling survives neither detection-threshold changes nor Kolmogorov-Smirnov testing, and surrogate signals reproduce it. The constructed null is "generic thresholded noise," destroyed only by a genuine SOC mechanism; the claim-side evidence fails to distinguish them.
- **Stability**: The verdict "power-law scaling" is shown unstable along two perturbation directions: the detection threshold (the measured exponent is not invariant under moving it) and the analysis class (regression shape vs KS-tested distribution). A scaling claim that flips sign of evidence under these perturbations carries no stability certificate.
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: The analytic half shows spurious power laws are a generic property of the thresholding operation itself (a single-channel marginal property), so observing the shape in LFP peaks licenses no inference about collective (joint) organization — exactly the excess the SOC claim needs and does not yet have.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The analytic result: spurious power-law scaling provable for stochastic processes without criticality — a mechanism-level construction of the null, not merely a surrogate demonstration; this is the same witness-system pattern as 2001.06408's counterexample but aimed at an empirical procedure.
- The positive control: the same rigorous analysis applied to a known-SOC artificial network unambiguously confirms its true power law — showing the test discipline CAN detect real SOC, i.e. the null is not a universal acid.
- The Timmer-lineage move transplanted to avalanche analysis: rejection/observation asymmetry between graphical evidence and distributional tests, arriving independently in neuroscience.
