## 2606.10295 --- Hohmeier, Fraiman & Moosmueller (2026)
**"k-Nearest Neighbors in Gromov--Wasserstein Space"**
arXiv: 2606.10295 | stat.ML

**Domain(s)**: TDA

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Matching**: the entire classifier operates on GW and fused-GW distances — nearest-neighbor search is itself an assignment problem over equivalence classes of metric measure spaces, so every prediction instantiates the Matching machine as its geometry. fGW adds node features alongside internal distances.

- **Stability**: universal consistency of the GW-k-NN classifier — the statistical-guarantee form of robustness: as sample size grows with k/n → 0, classification risk converges to the Bayes risk on the space of (weak) isomorphism classes of finite mm-spaces. Consistency proven *on the quotient space* (mod weak isomorphism), which is exactly where GW is a metric.

- **Null hypothesis** (weak): quotienting by weak isomorphism before doing statistics destroys label-irrelevant representation structure; the guarantee is stated only after this structure-annihilating identification.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. First corpus entry giving a *learning-theoretic* guarantee inside Matching-space: not stability of an embedding or of a distance value, but convergence of downstream classification risk to optimal, on the GW metric geometry itself.
2. The quotient-by-isomorphism move made load-bearing: consistency requires working on equivalence classes where GW separates points — a concrete demonstration that the Matching machine's metric properties are prerequisites for statistical inference on it.
3. No chain complex, parameterized homology, or joint-vs-marginal content; numerical section is standard benchmark validation.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: GW/fGW literature (Peyré et al., Titouan et al. for fGW), classic k-NN universal-consistency theory (Stone/Devroye lineage). No cross-domain engagement.
