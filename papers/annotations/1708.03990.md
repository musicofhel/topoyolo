## 1708.03990 --- Ashikaga & Asgari-Targhi (2017)
**"Locating Order-Disorder Phase Transition in a Cardiac System"**
arXiv: 1708.03990 | q-bio.QM

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems, neuroscience, information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Parameterized homology**: Channel capacity, mutual information, and transfer entropy are computed as spatially-resolved invariants — a profile over components of the cardiac system rather than a single number. The wavebreak locations that initiate ventricular fibrillation are exactly the loci where this invariant profile changes character; the order-disorder transition is *located* by reading the invariant field, the same move as tracking a topological invariant across a filtration parameter and calling the jump.

- **Null hypothesis**: The disordered regime (fibrillating dynamics) serves as the destroyed-structure reference: capacity/MI/TE between driver and tissue are evaluated against what survives when coherent wave propagation is lost. Order vs disorder is a two-regime comparison against degraded communication structure.

- **Stability**: The claim is predictive robustness — the located wavebreak sites are stable enough to be interventional targets (ablation planning), i.e. the invariant's spatial extrema persist under the system's own driven fluctuations long enough to act on.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Spatialization of channel capacity: capacity used not as a communication limit but as a *field over the components of a driven dynamical system*, whose spatial structure predicts where an order-disorder transition nucleates.
2. The single-driving-component architecture: one pacemaker-like driver vs many tissue components makes joint-vs-driver information flow the discriminating quantity — closer to directed information flow (transfer entropy) than to any of the six machines' canonical forms.
3. No topological machinery at all: the "phase transition" is spatiotemporal order/disorder of excitation waves, not a change in homology or an optimization landscape; only the invariant-profile-locates-transition pattern transfers.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Shannon channel capacity, mutual information, transfer entropy (communication analysis applied to physiology); analogy to phase-transition language is explicit in the title but no TDA/statistical-mechanics literature is engaged. Siloed on the cardiac side; the Rosetta bridge (capacity-as-order-parameter for a dynamical transition) is ours, not theirs.
