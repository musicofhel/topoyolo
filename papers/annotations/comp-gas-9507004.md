## comp-gas/9507004 — Palus (1995)
**"Detecting nonlinearity in multivariate time series"**

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: Extends the surrogate null to the multivariate setting: the tested null is a *multivariate* linear stochastic process, and the surrogates must destroy cross-variable dependence while preserving each variable's univariate statistics — a choice of what to destroy and what to preserve, now with a joint/marginal split across variables.
- **Joint-vs-marginal (composite systems)**: The test statistic is built from redundancies at several orders: linear redundancy (marginal/Gaussian-level dependence) vs. general redundancy (joint dependence beyond it). Testing specific dependence structures between groups of variables while controlling the linear part is exactly a joint-vs-marginal excess measurement embedded inside the null machinery.
- **Stability**: The linear-redundancy controls exist because imperfect surrogates produce spurious rejections — the method explicitly stabilizes the verdict against a known artifact of imperfect null construction.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Multivariate surrogate nulls with redundancy-order bookkeeping: separating spurious rejections caused by finite-sample/imperfect surrogates (visible in *linear* redundancy) from genuine nonlinearity (visible only in higher-order redundancy).
- Demonstrated on coupled linear/nonlinear synthetic systems and on real meteorological and physiological multivariate records — an early joint-vs-marginal null protocol predating the modern PID/permutation-consistency lineage (2005.06573).

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Univariate surrogate data testing (Theiler et al. 1992); redundancy-based information measures. Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
