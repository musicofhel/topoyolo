## 2506.04016 --- Rançon, Rançon, Ivek & Balog (2025)
**"Dreaming up scale invariance via inverse renormalization group"**
arXiv: 2506.04016 | cond-mat, cs.LG

**Domain(s)**: statistical physics, machine learning

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Stability**: The success criterion is not sample fidelity but persistence of structure under iterated coarse-graining — generated configurations are fed back through a real-space RG and must reproduce nontrivial RG eigenvalues. The network is certified by what survives the transformation, the atlas's stability criterion applied to a generative model.
- **Parameterized homology**: Scale invariance is verified across lengthscales: susceptibility, heat capacity, Binder ratios are checked to scale correctly as the (implicit) resolution parameter varies — the generated ensemble carries a consistent family of scale-indexed observables.
- **Null hypothesis**: The inversion task is *formally impossible* at the level of configurations (coarse-graining is many-to-one), so the correct reference is the destroyed-information null; learning succeeds probabilistically against that null, and the depth ablation ("multiple layers offer no significant benefit") is itself a complexity-null control showing three parameters suffice.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Direction reversal: every prior rg-dl paper here coarse-grains data (1410.3831, 2405.17538) or training dynamics (2504.12700); this one *inverts* RG to generate microscopic ensembles from coarse-grained states.
- The minimal-model result: universality of the critical distribution is capturable by local rules with three trainable parameters, fractal-rule style — a strong compression statement about what RG-relevant structure actually contains.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Real-space RG (Kadanoff block-spin lineage), 2D Ising criticality, RG eigenvalues/relevant operators, probabilistic inversion of many-to-one maps.
