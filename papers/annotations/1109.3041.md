## 1109.3041 --- Decelle, Krzakala, Moore & Zdeborová (2011)
**"Asymptotic Analysis of the Stochastic Block Model for Modular Networks and Its Algorithmic Applications"**
arXiv: 1109.3041 | cond-mat.stat-mech

**Domain(s)**: Statistical physics, information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Null hypothesis**: The undetectable phase IS the null made physical — above the detectability threshold the planted community structure carries no information extractable from topology alone, and inference from a sparse graph is provably no better than guessing from the marginal group sizes. The Kesten–Stigum-style detectability/undetectability transition is the canonical statement of "destroy the structure, lose the signal": the random-graph reference and the structured model become observationally indistinguishable.

- **Stability**: Cavity method yields the asymptotically exact phase diagram: a detectability/undetectability transition (second-order, at the KS bound for equal group sizes) and an easy/hard (first-order, condensation) transition. Which phase the instance sits in determines whether any algorithm can recover structure — stability of the inference problem itself, parameterized by average degree and coupling strength.

- **Matching**: The translated belief-propagation algorithm assigns nodes to groups maximizing overlap with the planted partition — approximate soft matching recovered asymptotically optimally inside the easy/detectable phase; the hard phase marks where the greedy/BP matcher's achievable overlap separates from the Bayes-optimal one.

- **Joint-vs-marginal excess** (instrumental): Detectability is precisely the question of when joint structure (planted block partition) exceeds what marginals (degree sequence alone) reveal — below the transition the excess is information-theoretically absent even though generatively present.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. The detectability/undetectability transition itself: a sharp, computable boundary for when network structure is inferable at all — the SBM analogue of the KS bound, and the anchor for every later community-detection threshold result (`1905.10031.md` proves memory-bounded algorithms fail strictly inside this framework).
2. The easy/hard separation: statistical-to-computational gap stated as two distinct transitions rather than one — structure can be known to exist while being exponentially costly to find.
3. Constructive payoff: the same cavity analysis translates directly into a practical BP algorithm, tested on real-world networks — phase diagram to deployed algorithm in one paper.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Cavity/spin-glass method (Mézard–Parisi lineage), earlier non-asymptotic SBM work by the same authors, belief propagation from coding/inference practice. Explicit statphys↔inference bridge; the KS connection is made via the reconstruction-problem literature. No TDA content.
