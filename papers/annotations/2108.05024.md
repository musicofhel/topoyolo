## 2108.05024 --- Grigoryeva, Hart, Ortega (2021)
**"Learning strange attractors with reservoir systems"**
arXiv: 2108.05024 | math.DS

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Matching**: The central theorem is a correspondence result: for generic observations h of an invertible dynamical system, a randomly generated linear state-space representation carries an embedding of the full phase-space dynamics into the reservoir state space — Takens' Embedding Theorem recovered as a special case of generalized synchronization. The "assignment" is the synchronization map ϕ (a topological conjugacy between the driven reservoir dynamics and the source system), which solves the correspondence between reservoir states and source states point-to-point and invertibly on the attractor.
- **Stability**: Generalized synchronization holds because the driven reservoir's conditional dynamics contracts transversally to the synchronization manifold; that contraction is what makes the conjugacy well-defined and continuous — i.e. the correspondence between reservoir state and source state is robust to initial-condition perturbation of the reservoir. (Depth-limited: abstract asserts conjugacy; no quantitative modulus of continuity visible at abstract level.)

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The identity claim: embedding IS synchronization. Takens' delay-embedding theorem is subsumed as a corollary of a generalized-synchronization statement about random linear state-space systems, so two literatures previously treated as separate tools (attractor reconstruction vs. reservoir-computing GS theory) become one object with one proof technique.
- The direction of generality runs from machine learning inward to classical dynamics: the reservoir result implies the dynamical-systems theorem, not vice versa — an ML-architecture fact doing theorem-proving work for math.DS.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Takens (1981) and the embedding-theorem lineage; reservoir computing / echo-state networks (Jaeger line); generalized synchronization in drive-response systems. No acknowledgment of TDA, QEC, or optimal transport.
