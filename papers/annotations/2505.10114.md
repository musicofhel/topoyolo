## 2505.10114 --- Lee, Kuklinski, Timme (2025)
**"Extreme Synchronization Transitions"**
arXiv: 2505.10114 | nlin.AO

**Domain(s)**: dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: This paper IS a null-hypothesis result aimed at classification itself: an "extreme synchronization transition" looks exactly like a discontinuous/explosive phase transition, but occurs at finite N and is therefore a multi-dimensional bifurcation, not a genuine thermodynamic-limit transition. The destroyed structure is the standard inference "abrupt jump ⇒ phase transition"; the complexified-Kuramoto mechanism shows the alternative explanation and gives a sharp test case for transition-vs-bifurcation classifiers.
- **Stability**: Analytic explanation of the jump mechanism (order parameter leaping from ~N^{-1/2} to ~1 immediately past critical coupling) via bifurcation analysis of the high-dimensional oscillator system — robustness of the almost-completely-ordered state is what makes the transition "extreme."

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- A named, mechanistically explained class of finite-N transitions that masquerade as explosive phase transitions — a cautionary counterexample class for any pipeline that infers phase transitions from order-parameter jumps.
- Transfer claim to percolation processes and other coupled-oscillator families suggests the phenomenon is structural, not Kuramoto-specific.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: explosive/s discontinuity literature, experimental observations motivating the model class, percolation theory. Explicitly positions against the thermodynamic-limit definition of phase transition — a rare authorial statement of the null distinction. No TDA, QEC, OT.
