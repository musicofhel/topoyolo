## 2005.06573 --- Rindt, Sejdinovic, Steinsaltz (2020)
**"Consistency of Permutation Tests for HSIC and dHSIC"**
arXiv: 2005.06573 | math.ST

**Domain(s)**: Information theory, machine learning

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The entire paper is the rigorous anatomy of one null: joint independence. The permutation test operationalizes the null by destroying the dependence link while preserving each marginal — randomly permuting the data rows severs the coupling between variables while leaving every marginal distribution byte-for-byte intact. The p-value is the rank of the observed dHSIC statistic within this sampled null distribution, so the test literally measures how far the observed joint structure sits inside the ensemble of marginals-preserved surrogates.
- **Joint-vs-marginal excess**: HSIC/dHSIC is the measured excess itself — the squared norm of the cross-covariance operator in the RKHS, which is zero if and only if the joint factorizes into the product of marginals. The paper's consistency theorem is a statement about this excess: under any fixed alternative the statistic converges to a positive constant (the population excess persists), while under random permutation its distribution collapses to zero in probability (the excess of a destroyed link vanishes asymptotically). Power → 1 is exactly the separation of these two limits.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- Closing the open question of Pfister et al.: permutation tests for HSIC/dHSIC are consistent, proved by elementary means (statistic → positive constant under alternative; → 0 in probability under permutation) rather than the proposed proof strategy.
- Type-1 error control extended to non-continuous data, with two explicit handling schemes for ties.
- Practical guidance on selecting the number of permutations, with vectors-of-permutations rather than individual permutations.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Kernel independence testing lineage (Gretton et al.), Pfister–Bühlmann–Schölkopf–Peters dHSIC, permutation-test theory (Hemerik & Goeman, Pesarin), mutual-information independence testing whose proof technique is transplanted. Applications named in graphical modeling and survival analysis. No connections to TDA, QEC, or dynamical systems.
