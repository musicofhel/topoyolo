## 1906.05212 --- de Mello Koch, de Mello Koch & Cheng (2019)
**"Is Deep Learning a Renormalization Group Flow?"**
arXiv: 1906.05212 | cs.LG, cond-mat.stat-mech

**Domain(s)**: machine learning, statistical physics

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The paper's whole diagnostic apparatus is a skeptical control on the rg-dl lineage's founding claim: hidden-visible correlation functions of an RBM trained on Ising configurations are compared against the correlators of an actual RG treatment, and "RG-like" is what survives that comparison — with the explicit finding that the same observables also exhibit *important differences* between RG and deep learning. The null ("deep learning is just RG") is constructed precisely so it can fail partially.
- **Stability**: RG-like coarse graining is diagnosed as persistence of correlator structure under the layer/scale correspondence — the hidden-visible correlators play the role of observables tracked across the flow, and their RG-like patterns are the stable signal.
- **Parameterized homology**: Single RBM layer ↔ single RG step gives the same scale-as-parameter reading as 1410.3831, but used as a *test bench* rather than claimed as an identity.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The correlator diagnostics themselves: correlation functions between hidden and visible neurons proposed as an operational test for RG-like coarse graining, rather than architectural analogy — this converts the Mehta–Schwab claim from a mapping into a measurable hypothesis.
- The partial refutation: numerical experiments show RG-like patterns AND important differences between RG and deep learning in the same observables. The lineage's first entry whose headline result is a qualification of its founding claim.
