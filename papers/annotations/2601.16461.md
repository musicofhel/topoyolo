## 2601.16461 --- Yadav, Song, Shkel & Özgür (2026)
**"Log-Likelihood Loss for Semantic Compression"**
arXiv: 2601.16461 | cs.IT

**Domain(s)**: Information theory

**Abstract machines instantiated**:

- **Matching**: The paper re-poses the rate-distortion problem with the distortion measure itself supplied by a conditional distribution P_{X|U}: a reconstruction U is scored by −log P_{X|U}(x|u), i.e. by how well it soft-matches the source through a prescribed generative channel. The resulting R(D) is still the constrained soft-matching functional of the Blahut–Arimoto lineage (`blahut-arimoto-1972.md`) — minimize I(X;U) subject to an expected cost on the coupling — but the coupling's cost is now a cross-entropy against a generative model rather than a pointwise distance. The optimal assignment is "find the cheapest semantic latent from which the source is plausibly generated."

- **Null hypothesis** (weak): The paper's structural results are reduction statements: the log-likelihood R(D) is characterized via its connections to (i) lossy compression under log-loss and (ii) classical arbitrary-distortion RD. Each connection instantiates one formulation as the computable reference/norm against which the other is measured — the same null-comparison role R(D) plays in `2204.01612.md`.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
1. Distortion as negative log-likelihood under a prescribed semantic channel P_{X|U}: the reconstruction is a *representation* from which the source is probabilistically generated, not an approximation of it — this inverts the usual direction of the test channel.
2. Unification result: the same functional interpolates between log-loss RD, classical RD with arbitrary distortion measures, and rate-distortion with perfect perception — i.e. the perception-constrained problem of Blau–Michaeli / Theis–Wagner (`2104.13662.md`) appears as a boundary case of one family.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Classical RD theory; log-loss compression; the rate-distortion-perfection/perception line (Blau & Michaeli's perfect-perception formulation). No cross-domain awareness beyond information theory.
