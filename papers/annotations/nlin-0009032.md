## nlin/0009032 — Timmer (2000)
**"What can be inferred from surrogate data testing?"**

**Domain(s)**: Dynamical systems

**Abstract machines instantiated**:
- **Null hypothesis**: The paper is the null machine's epistemics stated at maximum sharpness. A surrogate test can only falsify its own explicitly constructed null (e.g. "linear Gaussian stationary process"); a rejection licenses exactly that negation and nothing more. Using a positive surrogate result to "confirm" a specific nonlinear inference conflates rejecting one null with verifying a favored alternative — an inferential step the machinery never performed.
- **Stability**: The critique is a statement about where inference is stable under perturbation of the true process class: the conclusion "nonlinear" is not stable under moving the truth within the complement of the null (a non-stationary linear process rejects the same null). Only the rejected-null boundary, not the inferred alternative, is robust.

**What is genuinely new (not reducible to shared abstraction)**:
- The one-page sharpening of what a surrogate rejection entails: the asymmetry between falsification of a constructed null and confirmation of the intended hypothesis is made explicit for the entire surrogate-data literature.
- Methodological anchor for the whole null-surrogate lineage (Theiler 1992 onward): every downstream fix — multivariate redundancy controls (Palus 1995), power-vs-non-stationarity analysis (Timmer 1998) — responds to the gap this note names.

**Connections the authors acknowledge**: Surrogate data testing as practiced in nonlinear time series analysis (Theiler et al. 1992 lineage). Abstract-only provenance — depth-limited.
